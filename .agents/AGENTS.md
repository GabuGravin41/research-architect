# Style & Pedagogy Rules for the Discovery Curriculum

This project is written for the top 0.01% of students (e.g., Olympiad medalists). To respect their time and intelligence, we must adhere to strict writing standards that mimic peer-reviewed literature, elite monographs, or notebooks of classical physicists and mathematicians.

## 1. Tone and Voice (Eradicate "AI-Speak")

* **No Meta-Commentary:** Never write sentences explaining *how* the student will learn. 
  * *Bad (AI style):* "We will discover these laws in order, not by memorising them, but by seeing what they predict."
  * *Good (Intellectual style):* "These three laws form the axiomatic foundation of mechanics. We begin by examining the behavior of a particle subject to zero net force."
* **No Childlike Hand-Holding:** Avoid phrases like "Before we begin," "Sit with these questions," or "Write in your notebook." Give the mathematical setup directly.
* **Concise and Direct:** Let the structure of the puzzle and exploration do the teaching. Keep explanations brief, mathematically rigorous, and sober. No hype or exclamation marks.

## 2. Mathematical & Scientific Rigor

* **Axiomatic Clarity:** Introduce definitions and notations early and use them consistently. 
* **Olympiad-Grade Databases:** Problems must be drawn or inspired by International Olympiad databases (IMO, IPhO, IChO, IOI). They should require structural insight rather than tedious computation.
* **No Hype on Open Problems:** Present unsolved problems or deep theories (like P vs NP, Quantum entanglement, or Cauchy's functional equation) through their precise constraints. Never treat them as magic.

## 3. Diagram Standards (TikZ & PGFPlots)

* **Precision Geometries:** All coordinate points, vectors, and angles in TikZ must be mathematically correct and aligned.
* **Minimalist Aesthetics:** Use clean, professional color palettes (slate, navy, gold). Avoid cluttering diagrams with excessive labels or neon colors.
